# Endless Vortex — airtime prototype

Stay airborne in the enlarged wind tunnel. The build occupies **3x3 parcels (48x48 metres)**, centered at x/z 24. Arena height, wind volumes and floor spawn are uniformly scaled 1.5x from the 2x2 prototype. The orchard visual keeps its aspect ratio and is scaled to fit the 48 m scene. The central air tunnel lift and tangential vortex rotation activate from 1.8 metres upward; inward/outer containment pull, vortex lift and the broad updraft remain paused. Native steering, jumping, gliding, orbiting leaf platforms and golden recovery orbs remain active. Wind-plane effects remain removed, and the original looping theme music is active again.

## Active behavior

The altimeter measures height above the arena floor. Local-player overlap with the reset trigger plane ends the current attempt, saves its airtime, resets the live timer to zero and rearms the next jump. The trigger has no solid collider; its 0.01 cm plane is placed 0.01 cm above `ARENA.floorTop`. To cover mobile clients that miss the thin trigger event, the local player's estimated feet position also detects a downward crossing of that exact plane; merely being near or below the floor does not trigger a reset. Other players' overlaps do not affect the local attempt. The animated lava mesh stays visual-only and non-colliding.

Fallen rocks wait their configured respawn delay, then rise smoothly back to their home height over 0.8 seconds; collision returns when the rise finishes. Their idle hover is capped at 11 cm. The lava emission peaks at intensity 1.4.

Nearby players receive small multiplayer events when a local avatar steps on a rock, lands on a leaf, or collects an orb, so those state changes play for everyone. Orb indices use stable positions on every client; nearby-player clearance can delay a bubble's appearance locally, and leaf spawn clearance is evaluated from the visible nearby-player positions. Only state transitions are sent, not per-frame transforms. Orb lift, audio, airtime, and pickup totals remain local to the player who collected it. The SDK message bus does not retain a current obstacle snapshot for late joiners, so exact late-join convergence still needs a live two-client test.

- `orchard.glb` is the static environment at (23.75, 0, 24.25) and uniform 1.31 scale, 1.2 times larger while preserving its proportions. Its former 41.5 x 48 m footprint is now about 49.8 x 57.6 m. Visible meshes provide physics and pointer collision; invisible meshes do not collide, and the embedded animation is not played. Three visual-only `endless-vortex-wind.glb` layers run their embedded `Animation` loops at 3 m nominal vertical intervals. Each gets a one-time random position offset up to 20% of the model scale horizontally and 20% of the layer interval vertically, plus a 0.8–1.2 playback speed so its animation gradually drifts ahead or behind the other layers. Every layer has collision disabled, and the GLB material alpha is scaled to 70% for a more translucent effect. The generated wind model replaces the old procedural wind-plane effect. Airtime boundaries remain radius 21.6 m and height 30 m.
- Both blue lava centerpiece meshes are 1.2 scale about their shared center and remain non-colliding.
- The blue lava centerpiece uses two GLBs split from the original two-material model: the base keeps its authored gray material, while the lava primitive receives `assets/materials/blue-lava-molten.png`. The lava albedo UV offset slowly travels in a circular loop and its blue/cyan emission pulses at 12 updates per second; both meshes remain stationary at the arena center and have no collision. SDK 7.29 exposes UV offset and tiling but no UV rotation field, so this is circulating material flow rather than a true rotation of the UV coordinate frame. The source GLB and splitter are retained for reproducibility, while only the two split GLBs and texture are used at runtime.
- `rocks2.glb` is split into 158 independent GLBs so each rock has its own entity origin and its visible geometry supplies physics and pointer collision. Every rock is scaled uniformly to 1.2; contact bounds and sensors use the same scale. Ready rocks slowly bob upward by up to 7 cm at slightly different phases. Contact starts a one-second delay with an extra floating bob, then the rock drops over 0.3 seconds, stays hidden for 10 seconds, and respawns only when clear of the player. Timing is in `src/tuning.ts`; the original aggregate model and splitter script are excluded from published content.
- `AIRFLOW.centralOnly = true` temporarily enables only the central vertical wind volume: **32 force on desktop / 14 on mobile**. It is centered at x/z 24, spans 5.4 m across each horizontal axis and reaches y 24.375 m. Outside this volume, no continuous wind force is applied. Bubble contact can still give a small lift.
- Active vortex rotation uses spin 11. Saved inward pull 1.05, outer containment 2.94 and vortex lift 2.25 remain available. Set `centralOnly` to `false` in `src/vortex.ts` and rebuild to restore these and the broad updraft. Desktop/mobile multipliers remain 0.8/0.35; restoring broad lift would add 8/3.5 respectively. Pausing player wind does not pause platform orbiting.
- The old 48-entity wind-plane effect in `src/vortex-visuals.ts` remains dormant; the animated wind ribbon layer comes from the GLB and is visual-only.
- Normal client movement, jumping and gliding. No locomotion overrides or disabled glider.
- Original ground-level spawn. Airflow works immediately, without an attempt/start condition.

- Airtime is the result. Takeoff starts counting automatically; platform landing pauses it; jumping or stepping off resumes it. Landing on the dedicated floor-reset pad ends the attempt, saves its time and resets the live timer to zero. Moving away starts a new attempt; the personal best is retained. Rock contact and the general floor do not reset airtime.
- Standing adds no time. Leaving the playable arena pauses the run; returning resumes it when airborne, and the outside/re-entry intervals do not count. An unexpected teleport, lost connection after connecting, or sample gap over 0.5 seconds finishes the observed run; touch the floor-reset pad to rearm after those interruptions. Desktop pointer unlock pauses counting; time never catches up after suspension.
- Up to **48 leaf platforms** appear randomly at 80 allowed positions, at heights 4.5, 8.25, 12 and 15 metres. The lower bands have 16 positions each near a 14.5 m radius; the upper bands have 24 positions each near a 13.5 m radius, concentrating routes higher and closer to the vortex center. The first appears after 0.6 seconds, with further spawn opportunities every 0.45–0.70 seconds until the pool is full. Each uses the supplied `leaf1.glb` at uniform scale and its natural 3.86 × 1.96 m footprint, with a centered pivot and matching elliptical landing sensor. Leaves orbit at **8 degrees/second**, spin independently at 35–70 degrees/second and tumble through a restrained 40-degree range.
- The visible leaf GLB mesh is its physical collider; there is no separate disk or oversized invisible collider. Landing checks follow the same tilted top plane. Leaves are fully opaque with no overhead labels. Player clearance is checked at appearance and activation, and untouched leaves remain available without age-based expiry.
- The first landing starts a **2-second countdown**. It keeps counting after departure; repeated landings never reset it. A quick scale pulse warns throughout the countdown without transparency or labels. At zero, collision is removed and the opaque leaf visibly drops, then is hidden at floor height or after 2 seconds. Falling visuals cannot push a player or act as another landing surface. Unused leaves remain unaffected; replacements reuse the same pool.
- Leaf contact uses six 5 mm rays around the local player’s estimated foot point, aimed up/down and along each horizontal axis. Only a hit on that leaf’s visible GLB collider within 5 mm of the foot point starts its 2-second fall timer; contact can arrive from any direction. This removes the old inflated trigger boxes and avatar-radius allowance. The SDK exposes no separate avatar-foot-mesh contact event, so the foot point uses the player transform minus the calibrated 0.88 m foot offset. The same confirmed hit pauses airtime; all rays and collision decisions are local to that player.
- Visuals, colliders and support detection follow the same wind-wobbled orbit until collapse; falling visuals drop vertically. Position updates run every frame; warning styles update at 10 Hz. The fixed entity pool remains local to each player. A frame gap over 0.5 seconds clears platforms before restarting the spawn cycle, avoiding a large collider sweep on resume. Wind pauses on supported elevated surfaces. Native clients handle player contact; the only scripted teleport is the local ten-orb reward described below.
- The HUD uses the tutorial’s chamfered teal/gold frame. A right-side menu stacks airtime, best time, medals and nearby players, ending with a Flight Guide button. ORBS progress sits in its own top-center card: a complete gold ORBS wordmark centered with 30% of its height above the card. AIRTIME uses the same centered title overhang. The ORBS card also contains smaller gold count digits underneath, and ten larger, closely spaced orb progress icons. Medal durations use the blue digit font and a sprite seconds suffix, avoiding native numeric text. AIRTIME, MEDALS, NEARBY, ORBS and FLIGHT GUIDE use custom image lettering matching the tutorial buttons. Portrait screens place the side menu below the orb card; short screens show fewer nearby players. A blue crest distinguishes AIRTIME. New light-blue sprite digits and colon show the live timer as minutes:seconds.hundredths; nearby scores use larger minutes:seconds digits beside enlarged, framed player avatar photos. Winged rank insignia sit beside usernames and replace the overhead rank plates, preserving the 0–1,000 second run progression; regenerate their SVG/PNG artwork and alpha masks with `npm run rank:assets`. Bronze, silver and gold crest/ribbon emblems show earned and locked medal states. Flight Guide artwork is scaled to 57.5% while its tap target stays 44 px high. The altitude gauge is a slim horizontal card at the lower-right on landscape screens, with the teal/gold frame, an AIRTIME-style blue ALTITUDE title beside blue digit sprites, and a wider 16 px luminous blue/cyan gradient bar. The left side and lower corners stay clear. After 10 airborne seconds, altitude below 0.4 m triggers a pulsing red edge vignette that clears on recovery, pause or replay. At each 30-second mark, the HUD plays a 0.55-second wind flash while the synced avatar badge rotates a golden vortex around its digits for 2.4 seconds; no vortex animation plays in the fixed streak banner. Every 100 seconds through 1,000 seconds, a generated award frame briefly flashes at center and animates into the top streak slot; the highest frame repeats for later hundreds. Paused time does not advance effects, and replay rearms milestones. HUD animation updates are capped at 20 Hz. The ten transparent 640 × 214 milestone frames add about 2 MB compressed. See [asset list and exact generation prompts](assets/ui/modular/ASSETS.md). Browser fixture checks cover desktop and both phone orientations; native-client behavior still requires gameplay testing.
- Client detection is optional and defaults to weaker mobile lift when unavailable. Tap Lift: Mobile/Desktop to correct a misidentified client or compare tuning.
- Up to **24 golden recovery orbs** spawn around flight paths in height lanes 3.5, 7, 10.5 and 15 m, at radii 9, 13 and 17 m. Their smaller, semi-transparent gold bodies use a stronger pulsing emissive swirl with a camera-facing spinning `vortex.png` overlay; the former decorative highlight sphere is removed. Orbs orbit and bob gently. Spawns avoid the player, other orbs and leaf platforms.
- Contact with the local avatar pops an orb, including while the timer is paused/finished or the desktop cursor is unlocked. The pickup radius is 1.6 m and uses the avatar's approximate vertical capsule and relative motion between frames, so fast passes and upper/lower body touches register more easily. Teleport-sized position changes and invalid frame gaps are not swept through intervening pickups.
- Each runner's airtime appears as whole seconds in a ribbon beneath their winged rank insignia above the avatar name. Its billboard sits 65 cm above the Explorer's name-tag anchor to clear the taller artwork. The framed HUD timer shows seconds, best time and concise phase status; orb progress appears separately at top-center; the obsolete fixed red timer plate is removed. A shared billboard parent keeps every gold PNG digit aligned with the plate, with a small camera-facing depth offset so the numbers render in front. Unlit materials use explicit single-channel alpha textures for the plate and individually cropped digit sprites, preserving transparent cutouts without scene shading. Every client creates and synchronizes its own avatar-attached badge: the name-tag anchor, billboard, plate, digits, materials and visibility state are networked for nearby players. At each 30-second airtime mark a spinning vortex emblem animates above the badge for 2.4 seconds; its transform and visibility are synchronized so nearby players see it too. Bubble collection still powers the separate tenth-orb teleport reward. Airtime, medals, nearby players and the Flight Guide button share the side menu; the slim lower-right gauge shows altitude and a blue gradient fill bar.
- A pop requests a small upward impulse: **1.2 desktop / 0.7 mobile**, reduced as sampled upward speed approaches 3 m/s. At or above that sampled speed the orb still visibly pops, with no added lift. Pickups share a **0.8-second local lift cooldown**: additional contacts still pop, but cannot stack impulses. Each tenth local orb pop teleports that player instantly to the scene center (**x/z 24, y 30 m**). A 12-second return window gives room to fall back through the arena; the expected teleport displacement is excluded from interruption checks so airtime resumes with observed movement. This move is local-only and is not swept through bubble pickups. The threshold, destination height and return window are in `src/orb-reward.ts`. Orbs never award time or points directly.
- Popped orbs and their vortex overlay scale down and fade over 0.28 seconds while each pickup emits a slow-motion 3D spray of 48 tiny, high-emissive golden dust motes. Six more motes per orb join a bounded 60-particle ring that curls inward and keeps rotating tangent to the vortex center, building through ten pickups. On a successful tenth-orb teleport, the full 128-particle scene pool expands into a spinning cylinder from y=15.5–29.5 m, filling the upper vortex and fading after two seconds. A simultaneous 4.4-second logo-led splash sends 56 slow-moving gold/white/cyan UI particles across the view on desktop and mobile, showing the existing Endless Vortex logo instead of the congratulatory subtitle. Pickup motes shimmer, and transient sparks fade and hide after 3.2 seconds. Shine-material updates are staggered and rate-limited to reduce mobile cost. Orbs respawn after 5 seconds; untouched orbs relocate after 24 seconds. Each player has their own orb pool and pickup cooldown. There are no orb colliders, messages or network updates. All effect entities are pooled, with no wind planes restored.
- Pop audio reuses `assets/sounds/hit3.mp3` on one local audio source, with a 0.12-second sound interval; repeated playback restarts that source instead of layering sounds. **FX 50%** cycles through mute, 25% and 50%. The visible pop remains readable when muted.

The two lift areas are mathematical regions, not solid boxes. Each has one force source; editor wind scripts remain detached to avoid duplicate forces. The rejected containment box and green launch deck are not created. Old `world.ts`, `course.ts` and `ui.tsx` remain dormant. Earned airtime medals remain saved; this compact HUD displays airtime, personal best and standings.

## Launch

Use Node 24 or later. From this directory, start the existing records service in one terminal:

```powershell
node server/index.js
```

In another terminal:

```powershell
node node_modules/@dcl/sdk-commands/dist/index.js build --production --skip-install
node node_modules/@dcl/sdk-commands/dist/index.js start --skip-build
```

Or use `npm run build -- --production --skip-install`, then `npm start -- --skip-build`.

**Stop and relaunch an existing preview, and reload the project in Creator Hub** to load the new nine-parcel layout and scaled model. Both `scene.json` and the editor composite now have matching geometry, ground spawn and enabled portable experiences; stale launch-deck metadata was removed. Spawn should be at floor level near the new center.

1. Verify the lift label matches the device. Jump into the central air tunnel at x/z 24, then steer and glide out of its 5.4 m square footprint. Only upward lift should occur inside; outside, there should be no wind rotation, inward pull, outer containment or broad lift. Test both Lift settings. Platforms should continue orbiting.
2. Fly above the floor: airtime increases. Land on an orbiting emerald leaf: time pauses. Test riding it, walking toward its edge, and jumping off at different points in its orbit. Counting should resume on departure; wind resumes only on re-entering the central volume. Check for slipping, jitter, clipping and takeoff failures in each native client.
3. Leave a leaf unused for at least a minute: it should keep orbiting without expiring. Land on it: the leaf should pulse without overhead text. Step off and back on: the countdown must continue. At two seconds it drops and releases the player. Repeat across all eighteen leaves and all three height lanes, including brief edge landings and contacts while descending. Verify the other unused leaves stay solid, replacements appear, and all phases remain opaque. Check center/arena clearance and each visible edge and underside; the GLB itself should be the collider, with no oversized invisible disk. Confirm the decorative wind planes are absent while the wind still moves you.
4. Touch the floor: the final time remains visible. Take off again: only current airtime resets. Check live runners in the mid-right HUD list. Restarting the scene restores the personal best while the records service runs.
5. Unlock the desktop pointer or suspend the client: inactive time must not count. Leave and re-enter the arena while airborne: the run should pause outside and resume inside without crediting the gap. After a disconnect, unexpected teleport, or long sample gap, reach zero altitude before retrying.
6. Repeat with two identities on desktop and mobile. Record device names, client versions, FPS, landing/takeoff behavior and control problems.
7. Fly through a spinning golden orb while descending: verify the swirl overlay and stronger glow animate, the smaller translucent body and sprite scale down and fade, golden sparks emit from its origin, and it eventually respawns. Check motion at 30/60 fps and the expanded 1.6 m pickup radius during fast passes, including head/feet contact. Repeat with the timer paused or finished and cursor unlocked: contact should still pop. While rising quickly, the boost should be reduced or absent at the cap. Nearby contacts during cooldown should still pop without additional lift; neither the timer nor leaderboard gains seconds directly. On the tenth local pickup, verify one instant move to (24, 30, 24), the timer continues after the teleport discontinuity and during the 12-second return window, and ten further pops are required before the next move. Mute FX and repeat. Check that another player's pickups do not remove your orbs or move your avatar.
8. Touch several different rocks from above, beside, and while airborne: each contacted rock should bob gently for one second, then fall over 0.3 seconds independently, with its visible mesh and collision disappearing together. Verify nearby untouched rocks stay in place, and that a rock does not respawn inside the player.

For a phone or another computer, `127.0.0.1` points to that device. Set `TUNING.persistence.previewUrl` in `src/tuning.ts` to a reachable records URL and rebuild. For LAN testing, set `$env:HOST='0.0.0.0'` before `node server/index.js` and use the PC's LAN address on port 8787. Published scenes use `publicUrl`, which needs an accessible HTTPS service. No deployment or firewall changes are performed automatically.

Persistence reuses the existing HTTP service and `server/vortex-v1.json`. Without a service/configuration, the physical board reports offline/session-only records. The HUD list contains only players currently present in the scene. Local airtime is exact; remote airtime is estimated from observed avatar movement because the SDK does not expose another player's local platform support/timer state. Persistent global standings are client reported and unauthenticated, suitable for playtesting rather than verified competition.

## Checks

For an interactive visual check, run `npm run tutorial:preview` and open `http://127.0.0.1:8128/hud` for the score menu or `http://127.0.0.1:8128` for the tutorial. These fixtures render the actual JSX, include the guide button and audio, and support browser resizing. They do not simulate native Decentraland rendering, avatar portraits, collision or device FPS. Automated checks cover desktop, portrait and landscape phones, safe-area insets, live timer values, top-center orbs, guide placement and reward effects. Native-client verification remains necessary.

```powershell
node tests/run.cjs
node node_modules/typescript/bin/tsc --noEmit
```

The existing automated checks cover airtime transitions, frame-rate independence, interruption and independent player state; active vortex rotation, lift boundaries and force replacement; preserved glider settings with no wind planes; matching 3x3 scene/editor metadata; pooled leaf limits, GLB mesh collision, bounded orbital wobble, separation, bounds, support and suspension cleanup; blue lava texture/material-slot pulse and UV offset, fixed transforms and collision masks; looping music startup; unused-leaf persistence, two-second landing-triggered countdowns, repeated-contact protection, falling visuals and independent collapse; 158 separate visible-mesh rocks, full-bounds side contact, idle hover and hit bobbing, plus their one-second-delay and 0.3-second independent collapse; orb spin, swept capsule contact, respawn, obstacle exclusion, mute, small capped impulses, rapid-pickup cooldown and independent player state; and real HTTP persistence, ties and reset boundaries. These tests have not been extended to cover the 10-orb teleport reward. The fixtures inspect SDK ECS behavior; they do not simulate native collision physics, prove rendered smoothness on a device, or prove that a client carries the rider.

The earlier Codex in-app browser smoke test remained at Decentraland's 0% loading screen. It has not been rerun for this 3x3 iteration; current rendering and gameplay require a native-client check. The blue lava texture and material-slot pulse are covered by ECS fixture checks, but rendered texture quality and client performance still require visual verification in a native client.

Native desktop/mobile movement feel, moving-platform riding/takeoff, touch UI, multiplayer appearance and FPS remain unverified on physical devices for this iteration. If a client slips or fails to carry a rider, test a lower `orbitDegrees` first; a stationary collision surface with orbiting visuals would change the requested behavior and is not silently substituted here. Local platforms can make another runner appear to stand on an invisible surface; test with two players before expanding. Different lift settings do not establish device fairness. Mobile suspension protection depends on client pause/connection signals: an unreported background simulation cannot be reliably detected. Ten-player capacity is unverified.

Support detection combines native local-player trigger contacts, a downward physics ray and platform geometry with an approximate 0.88 m chest-to-foot offset. See the official [player-position documentation](https://docs.decentraland.org/creator/scenes-sdk7/interactivity/user-data) and [raycasting API](https://docs.decentraland.org/creator/scenes-sdk7/interactivity/raycasting). Confirm contact tolerances in each target client.
